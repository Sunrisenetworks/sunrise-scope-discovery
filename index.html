Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Scoping Tool

💡 How To Use This Flow

  • Lead with curiosity about their business, not your form — the questions below are sequenced so the easy, friendly stuff comes first.
  • Each card has a 💬 Try saying line — a natural way to ask the question out loud, instead of reading it like a form.
  • Budget and procurement now come later, once you've built some rapport and shown you understand their world — not in the first five minutes.
  • Tap into the Notes / Context area on the right to capture things in their own words.
  • When done, tap Save as PDF above to store a clean copy.

👋 1. Getting Acquainted

Just enough to keep notes straight — keep this brief and move on quickly. 💬 Try saying: "Before we get into it, mind if I jot down a couple of details for my notes — your company name and your name/title?"
The most important question of the call. Let them talk — this tells you what actually matters, and earns you the right to ask everything else. 💬 Try saying: "So tell me a bit about what's going on — what made you want to have this conversation today?"
Just enough scale to picture their environment — framed as curiosity, not a census. 💬 Try saying: "Just so I can picture it — roughly how many people are you supporting, and is that one location or a few?"
Shows you're trying to fit into their world, not barge into someone else's turf. 💬 Try saying: "Who looks after IT day-to-day for you — an in-house team, an outside provider, or a bit of both?"
Future plans shape what you scope today — easy, forward-looking, no risk to ask. 💬 Try saying: "Anything on the horizon — headcount growth, new offices, M&A — that we should be planning around?"
Map the buying circle early and casually — saves you from surprises later. 💬 Try saying: "Besides yourself, who else usually weighs in on a decision like this — IT, finance, leadership?"

🖥️ 2. Current IT & Security Architecture

Locate primary computing workloads to evaluate perimeter boundary protection.
Email and SaaS sprawl is where a lot of real-world risk actually lives.
Critical given hybrid work — this is where unmanaged risk usually hides.
Identify current defenses to map security integration opportunities.
"We have backups" and "we have tested, recoverable backups" are very different answers.
Frame this as learning their story, not auditing them — most clients have one. 💬 Try saying: "Has anything happened in the past year or two that gave you all a scare — a phishing attempt, an outage, anything like that?"

⚖️ 3. Compliance & Resilience Drivers

Determine compliance obligations to track mandatory engineering criteria.
Verify jurisdictional residency laws governing target corporate records.
Outside access is one of the most common breach paths — worth a direct look.
Measure disaster readiness and response recovery capabilities.
Insurers increasingly mandate specific controls — a great natural lead-in to scope. 💬 Try saying: "Do you currently carry cyber insurance? Sometimes the insurer's own requirements end up shaping what we'd recommend."

🚀 4. Scope, Budget & Next Steps

The needs-payoff question — anchors the whole proposal to their definition of a win, not yours. 💬 Try saying: "If we fast-forward 12 months and this was a clear win for you, what would that actually look like?"
Map desired solutions to scope the upcoming technical proposal.
Identify vendor preferences to leverage existing toolsets or skills.
Establish scheduling urgency to prioritize internal engineering resource deployment.
Confirms who actually needs to say yes before you build a proposal nobody can approve. 💬 Try saying: "Walking into next steps — besides you, who else needs to sign off before we can move forward?"
By now you've earned the context to ask this directly — it just helps you guide them properly. 💬 Try saying: "And just so I can guide you the right way — does this need to go through a formal procurement process, or is there flexibility to move directly?"
Ask this to right-size the proposal, not to qualify them out — frame it as being respectful of their time. 💬 Try saying: "So I don't waste your time with the wrong scope — is there a rough budget range already in mind, or should we build the case first and work backward?"
Address specific national constraints required for public sector entities.

Map structural visibility workflows into internal computing environments.

🌐 Deep-Dive: Network Infrastructure Scoping

Map data transit pathways between site footprints and central boundaries.
Evaluate the hardware foundation of the on-premises switching layer.
Determine wireless coverage scale and internal segment isolation layers.
You can't fix what you can't see — gauges how blind the current team is to issues.
